# Gallery 402 — design context for impeccable

Gathered 2026-09-02 from the codebase (`gallery/src`) and a short conversation
with Steve. Read by the `/impeccable:*` skills before they touch the UI.

## Design Context

### Users
Two visitors share one page. The first is a person — most immediately the
WebMCP Challenge judges, arriving from Devpost, giving the live site about
three minutes: they will read the hero, click one Buy, walk one room, maybe
open the tool panel. They should never hit a blank screen or a dead end, and
the paywall should feel real without feeling like a checkout. The second
visitor is an agent (ChatGPT's in-app browser, Chrome with WebMCP, a CLI) that
reads the tool surface rather than the pixels and pays on the person's behalf;
its human, in the docent's imagination, is "a daughter who loves boats". A
distant third: developers reading the source — the copy is not written for
them, and there are no console easter eggs.

### Brand Personality
**Warm docent.** Refined museum craft with a knowing wall-label wit — the
voice of a guide who likes the visitor and likes the paintings more. It
explains ("the box office is waking up — it sleeps when nobody has visited
for a while"), it never apologises or exclaims, and its jokes are small and
placed where a caption would be. Three words: hospitable, exact, unhurried.
Emotional goal: a judge should feel let in on something rather than sold to.
No confetti, no badges, no sound (Steve: none, ever), no AI-slop loading copy.

### Aesthetic Direction
A light museum room: the walls are a deep green (`--wall`) that recolours per
wing (lilac for the Impressionists, ochre for Van Gogh, indigo for Ukiyo-e,
oxblood for the Dutch cabinet); everything the visitor holds or reads is paper
(`--paper`) with brass accents (`--brass`, `--brass-2`), verdigris for "open"
and rope-red for "closed". Instrument Serif for display and wall labels, IBM
Plex Sans for the interface, a mono for prices, hashes and tool names. Print
and paper metaphors carry the state: tickets are stubs that print into a
ledger, the price list is an admission board with dotted leaders, doors are
framed mosaics that unblur when a ticket is held. Motion is sparse and eased
(240–900 ms), used to show a change of state — a row sliding into the tool
panel, a stub printing — never as decoration. Anti-references: SaaS dashboard
chrome, crypto-wallet neon, generic spinners, celebratory animation.

### Design Principles
1. **The state of the world is visible on the page.** Whatever the agent or
   the box office is doing, the person can see it — in the ledger, the pill,
   the tool count — and never has to wonder whether anything is happening.
2. **Copy is a wall label.** Short, specific, sentence case, one job per
   element. It says what is true right now and, when waiting, why.
3. **Paper holds state; the wall holds mood.** Anything the visitor owns or
   pays for is rendered on paper; the wall recolours to say where they stand.
4. **Motion marks a change, then gets out of the way.** Under one second,
   eased, respectful of `prefers-reduced-motion` (already guarded — keep it).
5. **Additive, never divergent.** The submission video is final; the live
   page may gain touches the video lacks, but nothing on screen in the video
   may change meaning.
